  \vspace{2em}\begin{abstract}
    Gravitational waves, ripples in the fabric of spacetime caused by accelerating masses, have become a cornerstone of modern astrophysics and cosmology. This paper provides a comprehensive overview of the mathematical framework underlying gravitational waves, starting from the Einstein field equations and progressing through linearized gravity to the generation and detection of gravitational waves. We explore the theoretical background necessary to understand these phenomena, including the properties of spacetime curvature and the behavior of perturbations in the metric tensor. Additionally, we discuss recent developments in gravitational wave astronomy, highlighting key discoveries and their implications for our understanding of the universe. The mathematical perspective presented in this paper aims to deepen our understanding of gravitational waves and their significance in the broader context of physics and astronomy.
  \end{abstract}
