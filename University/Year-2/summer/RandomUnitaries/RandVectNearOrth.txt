> Are there any theorems that can be stated informally as:
> 
> Let U be the unit ball in separable Hilbert space and let e be in
> U. "Most" vectors in U are "almost" orthogonal to e.
> 
> This is a variant of an idea in Roland Omnes, "Understanding
> Quantum Mechanics", Princeton, 1999, p74.

Here is a naive interpretation of your idea.  Probability
in infinite dimensional Hilbert space is not possible,
so let's work with N dimensional Hilbert space where
N is big, say l_2^N.  Let e = (1,0,...,0).  If one
picks a point x on the unit sphere at random, then
x.e = x1 gets close to a Gaussian with mean 0 and
variance 1/N when N is big.  More precisely, sqrt(N) x1
converges in law to a standard N(0,1) Gaussian as N->infty.

If you don't like this approximation, one can do fairly
explicit calculations to this effect.

Not only is it correct, but it is easy to prove.
The exact distribution is the symmetric Beta
distribution on (-1, 1) with density (for N>1)
C(1 - x^2)^{(N-1)/2 - 1).

This could be obtained from the properties of
independent Gamma random variables (the normal
distribution approach mentioned is of that type),
in particular that the total and the proportions
are independent, and the proportions for partial]
sums are Beta; all proportions are Dirchlet.

It can also be obtained by considering the radius
of the section left at height x.
